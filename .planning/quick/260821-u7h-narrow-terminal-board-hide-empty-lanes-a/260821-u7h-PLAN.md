<objective>
Make the board usable at ~125 columns: a `z` toggle that hides empty lanes, and
an honest window over the lanes that remain, holding the active lane in the
middle with two lanes either side of it.

Purpose: ten lanes across 125 columns is ~12 cells each — narrower than a ticket
handle, so the board is unreadable rather than merely cramped. Custom lanes mean
the lane count is open-ended, so this gets worse over time. On the user's own
board four lanes hold work, so the toggle alone solves it; the window is what
catches the case where many lanes are genuinely in use.

Output: two Model fields, one fit function and two pure helpers that state the
scroll rule, one status line, a key in the hint bar and the help screen, and
tests in the package's render idiom.
</objective>

**The scroll policy: a margin, not a centring**

The window becomes state, and the active lane keeps company on both sides. Two
small pure functions next to `boardFit`, free of the Model so a table test can
drive them — the same shape and the same reason as `laneFocusFit` in
lanefocus.go, whose doc comment is the model to follow.

`func scrolloff(perScreen int) int` returns `min(2, (perScreen-1)/2)`: how many
lanes stay visible either side of the active one. Comment why it is expressed
as a margin rather than as hard centring. Two each side is what the user asked
for and, at the five-lane fit of their 125-column terminal, it *is* centring —
but stated as centring the rule becomes impossible the moment fewer columns
fit. As a margin it degrades on its own: 2 at five lanes or more, 1 at three or
four, 0 at one or two, where it is simply a no-op.

`func windowStart(total, perScreen, start, cursor int) int` returns the first
visible position. Return 0 when `perScreen >= total` — everything fits, so there
is no window to move. Otherwise take `off := scrolloff(perScreen)` and the band
of starts that satisfy the margin: at most `cursor-off`, at least
`cursor+off-perScreen+1`. **Move `start` only when it falls outside that band**,
and then only to the nearest edge of it; a start already inside is returned
exactly as given. Finally clamp into `[0, total-perScreen]`, which is what makes
the ends behave as the user expects: near the first lane there is nothing
further left to scroll to, so the cursor legitimately sits at position 0 or 1
rather than in the middle, and the same mirrored at the last lane. The clamp is
last for a reason — say so in the comment: satisfying the margin must never win
over staying inside the board, because the only ways to do that are scrolling
past the end or padding the row with blank columns, and a blank column that
exists to flatter a scroll rule is a lie about the board's shape.

Comment the "only when outside the band" choice too, since it is the whole
user-visible point: the board today derives `start` from the cursor on every
frame, so every h/l press past the fold slides every column on screen and the
cursor never appears to move. Storing the window and leaving it alone means the
columns hold still while the cursor walks across them, and move only when it
runs out of margin.
